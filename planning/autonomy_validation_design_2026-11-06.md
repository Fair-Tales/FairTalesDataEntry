## Objective and decisions carried forward

Chris wants a pipeline accurate enough that accepted records do not require routine human approval. **Tune, independently prove the supported fields, then run with bounded automatic checking and permitted abstention.** Calibration and proof precede new cataloguing. Run both providers for relations and choose from measured results; there is no preassigned relation primary. Current frontier models from both providers are candidates, with exact identities, settings and costs frozen at execution time, rather than a restriction to the historical GPT-4o system.

The previous design had a substantive failure: most proposed confidence gates could not pass even with perfect output. This revision replaces those gates with resolvable lower-bound claims, reports rare-error ceilings without using them as release conditions, and introduces relation non-inferiority. **No blanket 95% confidence claim about the entire pipeline is made.** Each endpoint has a pointwise 95% bound; the former unspecified family-wise adjustment is removed. Exact numerical tolerances proposed below still need a research decision before proof, not adjustment after results.

The strongest existing asset is the original study's **human character/alias coding**, not page text. Imported page text is PDF-layer or model-derived text, and `validated=True` is an import flag, not evidence of human checking. Consequently the previous assertion that calibration needs no new truth was too broad. Inventory/attribute calibration largely reuses human evidence; independent OCR accuracy and the new relation validation need additional human evidence.

This supersedes incompatible claims in the earlier credit/autonomy plans, without editing those files. Preserve the protected 40 experiment, accounting, provider isolation, ramp, provenance and infrastructure controls. The base planning envelope remains **$199/provider/actual cycle** within each $200 allowance. One hundred books/month is a variable estimate, not a scheduling requirement. A top-up is considered in §7; unused money is preferable to an invalid experiment.

**Scope:** the 58 student-entered books remain excluded until the pipeline is tuned and validated on the 161 and Chris is confident in both models. His expectation of higher quality in the imported study cohort is reasonable, but the chapter describes research-team coding **and three citizen scientists**, not research-team-only coding. The separate 76 student-entered-book cohort is not the denominator for the 195/161 imported snapshot; the local evidence does not establish how the owner's 58 maps to those 76. No inferred reconciliation expands scope.

## 3. Provider comparison and bounded automatic validation

### Extraction and verification answer different questions

**Primary P / blind checker C:** for the existing extraction calibration, Anthropic proposes the output and OpenAI independently extracts from the original evidence, without seeing P, human references or adjudication labels. **Relations are a symmetric comparison:** run current frontier candidates from both providers, compare measured quality/coverage/cost on Tune, and freeze the release candidate and checker roles before Prove. Neither provider is designated primary for relations in advance. Use the same semantic extraction prompt, convention bundle and output schema where supported, with recorded provider-native wrappers. This measures provider difference under a common task; do not assume API or image-encoding identity. Each provider's text-dependent stages use its own OCR for the end-to-end comparison. A shared verified-text diagnostic may isolate reasoning errors, but is labelled diagnostic rather than end-to-end evidence.

Cross-check title/author/publisher, text and reading order, text/story presence, characters, aliases, attachments and all emitted character attributes. Relations require the new validation protocol; they remain experimental until its human-supported gates pass. Preserve raw surfaces and per-provider identity graphs. Equivalence is field-specific: Unicode/spacing rules for text, documented identity equivalences for metadata, evidence-backed character alignment, and separate lexical matching versus alias identity. Case folding must not hide invented-name errors; possessors must not disappear during identity matching. Convention differences are explicit outcomes, not automatically primary faults.

**Asymmetric verifier V:** the other-provider verifier (OpenAI in the original Anthropic-primary arm) then receives a specific primary claim and the relevant original image(s), with neighbouring pages/full-book context when required. It must return `supported / contradicted / insufficient_evidence`, exact supporting or counterevidence, page/region coordinates and a proposed correction when justified. Examples: “Is this printed name *Nbi* or *Nibi*?”, “Does this phrase refer to this character?”, “Which credited organisation is the imprint?”, “Identify printed regions omitted from this transcription.” Request counterevidence and an omission search, not merely a justification for P. A claim-only checker cannot discover an entity P never proposed; retain an independent page/region and character inventory check.

This is a serious alternative to repeating extraction: checking a disputed glyph against a larger original crop may be easier than generating a complete transcription. It may also anchor on P and repeat its error. **Neither superiority nor independence is assumed.** In Tune, compare C alone, V alone and a bounded hybrid on the same preselected development books, including ordinary agreements and known hard cases. Start with 20 Tune books chosen before predictions, expand only within the fixed Tune set and envelope. Run V across their eligible claims, including C/P agreements, so its shared-error detection is measurable. Synthetic deletions, invented-name substitutions and wrong attachments test mechanics/sensitivity, but never contribute to real-corpus accuracy or FNR estimates.

Recommended candidate to test: blind C on every admitted book; V on every identifier claim and on **all substantive disagreements plus predeclared risk triggers** (unusual names, uncertain glyphs, non-English spans, illustration lettering, implicit attribution, unexplained missing regions). V also checks a reproducible **5% random sample of remaining agreement opportunities**, selected by a stable run/input hash; these checks are automated. Freeze the actual triggers, sampling fraction, resource bounds and final acceptance rule before Prove. Random-check misses do not make the unchecked agreements correct; their residual risk must pass calibration. If V-only performs better, selection is permitted in Tune, but a blind extraction arm remains in the calibration experiment to measure agreement explicitly.

### Bounded resolution without a human

1. Deterministic checks establish schema validity, image/page completeness, source quote/offset validity, referential integrity and approved metadata equivalences. These prove structure, not the semantic truth of model evidence.
2. V examines disputed claims using evidence that can differ from the primary's crop/OCR. An uncorroborated model confidence score never decides acceptance.
3. If needed, permit **one adjudication request** against retained original evidence, with conflicting candidates anonymised/reordered and an explicit insufficient-evidence option. This can be a different prompt/model within either funded provider; it is a third *opinion*, not an independent gold judge. No third paid provider is assumed. Cap corrections at one candidate revision and one recheck; no repeated voting until agreement appears.
4. Accept a correction only through the **specific correction policy proved on held-out truth**, including its wrong-change rate. Otherwise abstain at the affected field and invalidate dependent outputs. Where truth cannot establish that policy, automatic semantic correction is not certified. An ambiguous alias can be retained as an unassigned surface; a disputed identifier cannot create an authoritative join.

The checker does not merge its extractions into canonical records, take majority votes as truth, or fill every gap. Its output is evidence and a validation disposition. The chosen extractor's initial proposal, all alternatives and the final policy decision remain distinct. Cross-family verification is a deployment component; it is never the independent human truth used to certify that component. Evaluate **final accepted risk**, including correct-primary values damaged by checker/resolver actions; a checker that produces more harmful edits than useful repairs fails regardless of disagreement volume.

### Recording disagreements through existing entities

Follow [CLAUDE.md #129 and the Field contract](../CLAUDE.md). Proposed declared fields on `Book`, `Page`, `Character`, `Alias` and AI-populated `Author`/`Illustrator`/`Publisher` are `analysis_provenance`, `analysis_run_ids` and a bounded `validation_state`/validation-run pointer. Store immutable full comparisons in a `DataStructureBase`/`Field` analysis sidecar with declared fields, references, form contract and stable document identity. No raw Firestore-dictionary shortcut.

Each field comparison records: target/entity IDs; P/C/V run IDs and value hashes; match/equivalence rule version; substantive/trivial agreement; disagreement type; evidence image/text hashes and region/span pointers; verifier verdict; proposed correction; resolution attempt/cost; final status/reason; certificate/policy version; applicability/coverage and reference-evaluation status where relevant. Book/field status must distinguish `auto_accepted`, `auto_corrected`, `abstained`, `experimental` and `legacy_unknown`. Do not set `validated_by` to a fictitious human or redefine an old human-validation flag silently.

Preserve `Page.extraction_evidence`, `Book.metadata_evidence_payloads`/`metadata_history`, `Character.detection_audit`, `Alias.provenance` and immutable alias-review history. Every record needs origin, **provider, requested/resolved model and convention version**, plus prompts/schema/settings, source hashes, run/request IDs and timestamps. Derived fields link all contributing sources. Human values use `not_applicable` for provider/model and retain their history; missing historical provenance stays explicitly unknown.

Use the entity layer's bundled/precondition update mechanism to commit accepted value, provenance, status and timestamp together. Ordinary separate Field assignments are write-through and can leave partial history. Validate identity before creating title-derived book IDs or author/publisher links; later renaming requires the existing controlled migration, not a casual title edit. This publication path and removal of existing UI approval gates require future implementation and qualification; this document does not claim today's app already runs unattended.

### What runs and what is scored

For each book, retain original input bytes and order; immutable references and their provenance; P's raw outputs; blind C's raw outputs; V/adjudication outputs when the tested policy invokes them; final accepted/abstained output; and metered attempts. Scoring never sends human truth to either model. Run the primary's real preprocessing, while preserving originals for counterchecks: sharing a mistaken crop with both providers can create shared error. Report shared-preprocessing versus original-image verification explicitly.

First score P and C independently against eligible reference, then their agreement, then V and the final policy. Define the unit per endpoint in advance (book identifier tuple, text-bearing page/span, known character/alias attachment); cluster all uncertainty at book/work level. Report numerator, denominator and reference tier for every metric, not just percentages. Use a fixed reference-positive denominator for recall, including missed outputs. For output-based groundedness show emitted count, empty-output count and recall alongside it.

For reference-evaluable, nontrivial opportunities, let:

- `E` = P is wrong under the frozen field-specific correctness rule; `K` = P is correct.
- `A` = P and C agree under the frozen substantive equivalence rule; `D` = substantive disagreement; `X` = unavailable/failed/incomparable checker outcome. These are disjoint. An unsupported reference is `U`, outside correctness denominators but inside coverage reporting.
- `n(S)` = count of opportunities in event `S` (or the explicitly stated weighted count). Do not silently mix unit types or reference tiers.

| Quantity | Definition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement rate on content | `n(A) / n(A ∪ D)` plus `X` rate over all eligible attempts | Similarity only; report trivial-agreement share separately over all field opportunities. |
| Correctness given agreement | `n(A ∩ K) / n(A)` on known truth | How often agreement predicts verified primary correctness; often called negative predictive value when disagreement is the alarm. |
| Shared-error risk among agreements | `n(A ∩ E) / n(A)` | Residual risk of trusting agreement; complement of the preceding quantity on the same known-truth denominator. Also report `n(A ∩ E) / n(eligible known-truth attempts)`. |
| **False-negative rate of agreement-as-validator** | **`n(A ∩ E) / n(E)`** | Fraction of genuine primary errors missed because the providers agreed. Report `X ∩ E` separately as blocked/unresolved errors. Detection by substantive disagreement is `n(D ∩ E)/n(E)`, not necessarily `1 − FNR` when X exists. |
| Disagreement yield | `n(D ∩ E) / n(D)` | Fraction of alarms finding a real primary error. |
| False-alarm rate | `n(D ∩ K) / n(K)` | Fraction of correct primary outputs that the checker unnecessarily challenges. Also report false alarms per 100 attempted books. |
| Wasted-alarm share | `n(D ∩ K) / n(D)` | Fraction of disagreements consuming resources without finding a primary error. |

Expand disagreements into **P wrong/C right; P right/C wrong; both wrong differently; both defensibly correct under multiple valid answers**. The last case diagnoses comparator/convention noise. Record unknown-truth disagreements separately; don't label them primary errors. In an agreement, shared correctness remains unmeasurable if the reference does not establish it. Both models omitting a reference-known alias is a shared recovery error, even if their emitted alias sets match perfectly.

For V, repeat the table with `pass / flag / abstain`. Let `I` mean V was invoked: **`FNR_V|I = n(I ∩ pass ∩ E)/n(I ∩ E)`**, and residual risk is `n(I ∩ pass ∩ E)/n(I ∩ pass)`. These describe the selected invoked population, not all books/claims. The all-claim Tune20 comparison supports a development estimate across its eligible claims; a broader estimate from selectively invoked checks requires frozen, nonzero inclusion probabilities and appropriate selection weights. Neither is a substitute for held-out policy evaluation.

For the complete deployed policy, define **`FNR_policy = n(E ∩ final_wrong ∩ accepted)/n(E)`** over **all eligible, known-truth primary errors**, including erroneous agreements that bypass V. Repaired or abstained primary errors are contained; final accepted errors are missed. Separately measure `wrong accepted / all accepted`, errors prevented or repaired per attempt, **correct-primary → wrong-final** changes, abstention and accepted coverage. Define a policy false alarm as any substantive claim that P is erroneous when P is correct, whether later cleared or not; routine scheduled checks that support P are not alarms. Report false-alarm rate over all known-correct P opportunities, plus correct outputs unnecessarily abstained or changed. An error corrected before acceptance is not a final error; an uncorrected error merely waved through by an adjudicator is. Publish book-level “any material error” rates alongside opportunity metrics.

Quantify checker costs: USD and latency per true error detected/corrected; paid checks and escalation dollars spent on `D ∩ K`; correct outputs turned into abstentions; harmful corrections; human minutes actually used, separated into Tune, independent proof annotation and optional Run audits. With zero detected errors, cost per finding is undefined, with total cost still reported. A high false-alarm checker can make autonomy unusable even when it is cautious. Compare final risk/coverage/cost against P alone and against blind C; select only from development results.

The calibration report must include all agreements, not only inspected disagreements. Scoring agreements against valid human character/alias/attribute reference supplies a shared-error denominator for those endpoints. Machine-derived page reference supplies only discrepancies/agreement, not verified OCR errors; relations need the new human pass. Report any available rare-name, non-English, stylised typography, illustration-lettering and implicit-attribution subgroup separately; an absent/unknown subgroup cannot receive a general certificate. No supplied measurement establishes the FNR yet: this experiment measures it where the reference supports truth.

**Required correlated-error strata:** (1) primary-provider-derived reference (`ocr`, cleaned `layer`, `skipped_wordless`), the most direct and potentially largest circularity; (2) shared OCR/preprocessing/crops/page omissions; (3) shared human or generated character inventory and alias table; (4) common task conventions/training errors; (5) same-family extractor/annotator or verifier. Report P/C agreement and apparent reference agreement separately in each available stratum. Empty/empty on `skipped_wordless` is specifically provider agreement with its own earlier decision, not human confirmation. Independent source-image human truth, not another model vote, is what can reveal this channel.

## 5. What proof establishes

Freeze the complete extraction/checking/correction/abstention policy, endpoints, provenance, normalisation, matching, weights, unknown handling, confidence methods, margins and cost bounds before unblinding. No favourable-seed selection, replacing failed books, tuning on proof, or optional extension after a miss. Historical labels exposed during development never become fresh proof. The relation protocol separately specifies the predeclared 50-to-200 annotation option and its inferential consequences.

**Per-field release:** the direct final-output quality floor and operational coverage pass; the reference supports exactly that claim; known structural/fixture regressions are absent; and the tested correction/publication/accounting path is the one released. Report unknown-truth best/worst-case sensitivity. Neither agreement, M1 nor matched-character agreement alone establishes complete identity or relation correctness. A qualified field need not wait for an unestablishable auxiliary validator-sensitivity claim.

**Confidence procedure.** Drop the former unspecified family-wise alpha. Use pointwise one-sided `alpha=0.05`, predeclared endpoints, and disclose that the joint probability that every interval covers is not 95%. Report all results, including failures. Comparing many variants and selecting a winner on proof is not permitted: choose on Tune; a newly selected proof winner needs independent confirmation. This restriction also applies to the two relation providers.

For the primary book-macro recall/agreement bounds, let `X_i` be each independent work's score in `[0,1]`, and `w_i` its fixed normalised design weight, summing to one. Related editions are one work. Set `q=Σw_i X_i`, `w_max=max(w_i)`. Define the lower bound `L` by

`KL(q || L) = w_max × log(1/alpha)`, with `0 ≤ L ≤ q`,

where `KL(q||L)=q log(q/L)+(1−q) log((1−q)/(1−L))`; `L=0` when `q=0`. At `q=1`, `L=alpha^w_max`. This conservative bounded-variable Chernoff bound allows arbitrary dependence **within** a work and different stratum means; it assumes independent works and frozen weights. Its basis is the bounded-variable Bernoulli moment-generating-function bound, convexity at weights `w_i/w_max≤1`, and weighted Jensen. It does not use a fitted within-book independence model. Sampling without replacement within the frozen pilot strata is no worse for this finite-population bound; transfer to future books additionally requires a comparable independent-work population. Report finite-pool and future-population interpretations separately.

With the nominal Prove weights `(63/121)/32` and `(58/121)/28`, `1/w_max=58.4138`; the perfect bound is **0.950008**. For 32 alias-positive works it is **0.910632**. These bounds are conservative: the character/attribute 0.90 floor needs observed macro score about **0.98001**; alias 0.90 needs about **0.99756**. Thus alias proof is still marginal, openly so. The analogous conservative **future-work** relation gate is even stricter: speaker>0.94 needs q>0.99750 at nominal effective 58.4138 works (q>0.99969 at 50); recipient>0.91 needs q>0.98508 (q>0.98932 at 50). Roughly four sections/work means one speaker miss normally defeats that future-work claim. This is why the relation study distinguishes resolvable finite-frame performance from a broader future-book certificate; it does not pretend human 200 restores 200 independent books. A 0.80 alias macro floor would permit an observed score near 0.95, but changing that tolerance is a research decision, not a statistical repair. Per-category 0.80 at 14 works is likewise only a perfect-case feasibility boundary. Publish these power limits before proof. Recompute actual weights/bounds when eligibility or work grouping changes.

Use stratified work-cluster resampling for secondary micro ratios, kappa and paired effects, preserving inclusion probabilities and whole works. Name these as approximate intervals, with numerator/denominator and number of independent works. For all-zero/all-perfect events, use an exact independent-book event bound or the bounded-score bound above; an all-zero bootstrap interval is not evidence of zero uncertainty. Relation primary inference follows its declared finite-frame section sampling design, including paired analyses only where a common-unit probability design supports them. Future-work relation analysis uses work clustering. Neither is replaced silently by item-iid intervals.

**Rare-error arithmetic, reported rather than gated:** with zero failures on independent representative books, `U=1−0.05^(1/n)`:

| Independent books | Upper bound on book-event error |
|---|---:|
| 60 | 4.8703% |
| 121 | 2.4454% |
| 161 | 1.8435% |
| 200 | 1.4867% |

A 1%, 0.5% or 0.1% upper ceiling needs **299, 598 or 2,995 zero-error independent books**, respectively. These numbers describe book events, never page error or attachment error without a declared within-book model. The original twelve-or-so unreachable gates are removed, not left as implicit blockers. All 161 still cannot establish those ceilings and is not an untouched holdout.

**Validator FNR is conditional evidence.** Keep `FNR_policy=n(E ∩ final_wrong ∩ accepted)/n(E)` and the §4 diagnostics. A proposed **FNR upper bound ≤5%** can be tested only when **n(E)≥59 independent verified error units** under the stated model; 59 with zero misses is a minimum, not a guarantee with misses. For a book-event analysis that means 59 independent error-bearing works. Otherwise report “sensitivity not established”; at n(E)=0 it is undefined. Show actual independent error-bearing works, opportunity counts and provenance. Synthetic errors test mechanics only. More runs on the same examples do not enlarge the independent denominator.

If a final-output gate passes while FNR remains unresolved, certify the measured final-output claim and explicitly withhold validator-sensitivity assurance. Do not claim that a passing agreement check itself proves correctness. Automatic corrections remain part of the final output scored against truth, including harmful correct-to-wrong changes.
