## Objective and what this supersedes

Chris's objective, verbatim:

> "100 books is a good estimate but it is going to vary a lot depending on work
> and life. Anyway the key bit is I want the pipeline to work well enough that
> human review is not required. One idea I had for the Open AI API credits was
> that we could use them to check/validate the primary pipeline and pick up any
> mistakes. But the key point is that the pipeline should be good enough to not
> need human validation (the validation is just for tuning the pipeline and then
> proving it is sufficiently accurate to run on its own)."

**Use OpenAI to find and validate errors in the Anthropic pipeline; calibrate that validator against the existing verified pilot; switch off routine review only for fields whose frozen end-to-end acceptance policy passes an independent sufficiency gate.** Calibration of the 161 usable books is the highest-value initial credit use: it needs no new annotation and can measure shared errors that new, unlabelled catalogue entries cannot reveal.

This supersedes the standing disagreement-review queue, review-hours throughput constraint, new-cataloguing-first order and steady monthly volume assumptions in [the previous credit plan](credit_utilisation_plan_2026-11-05.md). Its accounting, provider isolation, ramp, stop rules, infrastructure limits, provenance and protected 40-book experiment remain binding. Use **$199/provider/actual cycle** as the planning envelope within each $200 allowance; unused credit is preferable to an invalid experiment or an unsafe final request. One hundred books/month is a variable estimate, never a scheduling dependency.

The likely result is **uneven readiness across fields**, not a single accuracy score. The present reference can support useful OCR and known-positive character/alias calibration, subject to provenance. It cannot establish true alias precision, complete identity accuracy or speaker/addressee accuracy. Nor can 161 books alone establish very rare future-book error rates. These are evidence limits, not reasons to retain routine human validation forever: either narrow the certified output, obtain separately authorised missing evidence, or leave the affected field explicitly unproved. Do not describe partial certification as completion of Chris's full research objective.

## 2. Proposed meaning of “sufficiently accurate”

These are **research proposals for Chris to ratify**, not measured achievements or thresholds selected to fit existing scores. Apply them to accepted outputs in the certified input population, while also reporting errors and coverage across every attempted book. A correct-looking subset obtained by rejecting most books does not pass.

Use conservative one-sided confidence bounds, with family-wise 95% coverage across the predeclared release endpoints (§5). “≥” means the lower bound meets the floor; “≤” means the upper bound meets the ceiling. Where a metric cannot be measured, its gate is unavailable, not assumed passed. Costs below distinguish credit-funded measurement on existing references from missing human truth; none commissions new annotation.

| Field / downstream consequence | Proposed sufficiency requirement | Measurement and whether existing data can establish it | Incremental cost / missing evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Title, author, publisher identity**. A wrong identifier corrupts joins/deduplication and can attach every later result to the wrong work. | **≥99.9% correct accepted book-level identifier tuples**, with title/author/publisher reported separately; no automatic merge on a fuzzy name alone. Retain imprint versus publishing house and edition distinctions. Missing/ambiguous required identity means no authoritative join. | Exact identity match under frozen, evidence-backed equivalences; count any wrong tuple component as a book failure. Verify source credit/title/imprint regions, including reteller convention. Spreadsheet title/author may provide usable reference; imported publisher/illustrator AI lookup is not independent truth. Reuse already captured source regions. Why these differ: identity corruption affects entire joins, so it gets the strictest tolerance. Common OCR and character omissions propagate broadly, so both aggregate recovery and consequential-error gates apply. The provisional 95% alias recovery floor tolerates some incomplete lexical coverage while imposing a much tighter wrong-attachment limit; systematic undercoverage by language/name type still fails the subgroup checks. These numbers express downstream research loss tolerances, not model capability.

**Coverage proposal:** at least **95% of evidence-eligible opportunities accepted per certified field**, and at least **90% of in-scope books complete for all fields required by the declared analysis**, with lower confidence bounds. Report by language, content density, typography and explicit/implicit attribution where applicable; sparse or unrepresented strata stay uncertified. Compute both book-macro and opportunity-weighted coverage. Include failures, unsupported input and abstentions in all-arrival coverage, separately from the in-scope denominator. If the full analysis needs speaker attribution, its present coverage for *certified* use is zero; exporting experimental relations does not change that.

**Reference limits that cannot be normalised away:**

- Use the supplied usable-pool counts: **161 books, 90 alias-eligible / 71 zero-alias, 1,297 characters, 178 aliases, 5,582 image objects**. These are inventory facts, not counts of independent statistical trials or uniformly gold fields. The earlier 48% alias-zero finding was **93/195** in the student cohort; 71/161 is a different denominator. Lowercase descriptive expressions belong in scope. **0.447 reference alias precision is an incompleteness artefact**, not pipeline error.
- The supplied **193/276 books with empty story pages concern genuinely wordless content**, not assumed lost text. Do not revive the earlier inference that emptiness itself means missing transcription. Crosswalk IDs and field eligibility: do not apply 193/276 mechanically to the 161 subset. Earlier plans' different text-bearing eligibility descriptions require a manifest reconciliation using existing evidence, not an invented revised count.
- For OCR agreement, include a reference-known nonempty page even if both providers emit empty text: that is a shared omission. If either provider emits text on a genuinely empty page, measure the possible false-text outcome separately. Empty/empty on a known empty field is **trivial agreement**, reported separately, never credited to the substantive headline. Empty/empty with unknown truth remains unknown. Apply the same principle to alias/entity sets; a zero-alias reference is not proof that no aliases exist.
- Legacy `variants` mixed names and aliases. True dev20 explicit-alias recall is **6/10 across five books**, only a smoke test; no sufficiency threshold is fitted to it. Three dev20 books had undefined M1 because output was empty. Keep those denominators visible.
- [The reference-provenance audit](validation_harness_design_2026-11-04.md) distinguishes independent human text from imported PDF/OCR/cleaned text and AI metadata. Preserve already-supported corrections, including [the seven recovered-text cases](wordless_adjudication_2026-11-05.md), without treating their targeted selection as a random accuracy sample. Freeze a field-level `verified / partial_known_positive / weak_reference / unknown / not_applicable` mask before scoring. Only verified truth supports correctness claims; known positives support recall. Unknowns are not silently counted as correct or as checker errors.

## 3. OpenAI as validator, not a second publisher

### Extraction and verification answer different questions

**Primary P:** Anthropic proposes the complete output using the frozen primary pipeline. **Blind checker C:** OpenAI independently extracts the same fields from the original page evidence, without seeing P, human references or adjudication labels. Use the same semantic extraction prompt, convention bundle and output schema where supported, with recorded provider-native wrappers. This measures provider difference under a common task; do not assume API or image-encoding identity. Each provider's text-dependent stages use its own OCR for the end-to-end comparison. A shared verified-text diagnostic may isolate reasoning errors, but is labelled diagnostic rather than end-to-end evidence.

Cross-check title/author/publisher, text and reading order, text/story presence, characters, aliases and attachments. Check experimental language relations only as experimental outputs until their truth exists. Preserve raw surfaces and per-provider identity graphs. Equivalence is field-specific: Unicode/spacing rules for text, documented identity equivalences for metadata, evidence-backed character alignment, and separate lexical matching versus alias identity. Case folding must not hide invented-name errors; possessors must not disappear during identity matching. Convention differences are explicit outcomes, not automatically primary faults.

**Asymmetric verifier V:** OpenAI then receives a specific primary claim and the relevant original image(s), with neighbouring pages/full-book context when required. It must return `supported / contradicted / insufficient_evidence`, exact supporting or counterevidence, page/region coordinates and a proposed correction when justified. Examples: “Is this printed name *Nbi* or *Nibi*?”, “Does this phrase refer to this character?”, “Which credited organisation is the imprint?”, “Identify printed regions omitted from this transcription.” Request counterevidence and an omission search, not merely a justification for P. A claim-only checker cannot discover an entity P never proposed; retain an independent page/region and character inventory check.

This is a serious alternative to repeating extraction: checking a disputed glyph against a larger original crop may be easier than generating a complete transcription. It may also anchor on P and repeat its error. **Neither superiority nor independence is assumed.** In Tune, compare C alone, V alone and a bounded hybrid on the same preselected development books, including ordinary agreements and known hard cases. Start with 20 Tune books chosen before predictions, expand only within the fixed Tune set and envelope. Run V across their eligible claims, including C/P agreements, so its shared-error detection is measurable. Synthetic deletions, invented-name substitutions and wrong attachments test mechanics/sensitivity, but never contribute to real-corpus accuracy or FNR estimates.

Recommended candidate to test: blind C on every admitted book; V on every identifier claim and on **all substantive disagreements plus predeclared risk triggers** (unusual names, uncertain glyphs, non-English spans, illustration lettering, implicit attribution, unexplained missing regions). V also checks a reproducible **5% random sample of remaining agreement opportunities**, selected by a stable run/input hash; these checks are automated. Freeze the actual triggers, sampling fraction, resource bounds and final acceptance rule before Prove. Random-check misses do not make the unchecked agreements correct; their residual risk must pass calibration. If V-only performs better, selection is permitted in Tune, but a blind extraction arm remains in the calibration experiment to measure agreement explicitly.

### Bounded resolution without a human

1. Deterministic checks establish schema validity, image/page completeness, source quote/offset validity, referential integrity and approved metadata equivalences. These prove structure, not the semantic truth of model evidence.
2. V examines disputed claims using evidence that can differ from the primary's crop/OCR. An uncorroborated model confidence score never decides acceptance.
3. If needed, permit **one adjudication request** against retained original evidence, with conflicting candidates anonymised/reordered and an explicit insufficient-evidence option. This can be a different prompt/model within either funded provider; it is a third *opinion*, not an independent gold judge. No third paid provider is assumed. Cap corrections at one candidate revision and one recheck; no repeated voting until agreement appears.
4. Accept a correction only through the **specific correction policy proved on held-out truth**, including its wrong-change rate. Otherwise abstain at the affected field and invalidate dependent outputs. Where truth cannot establish that policy, automatic semantic correction is not certified. An ambiguous alias can be retained as an unassigned surface; a disputed identifier cannot create an authoritative join.

The checker does not merge its extractions into canonical records, take majority votes as truth, or fill every gap. Its output is evidence and a validation disposition. Anthropic's initial proposal, all alternatives and the final policy decision remain distinct. Evaluate **final accepted risk**, including correct-primary values damaged by checker/resolver actions; a checker that produces more harmful edits than useful repairs fails regardless of disagreement volume.

### Recording disagreements through existing entities

Follow [CLAUDE.md #129 and the Field contract](../CLAUDE.md). Proposed declared fields on `Book`, `Page`, `Character`, `Alias` and AI-populated `Author`/`Illustrator`/`Publisher` are `analysis_provenance`, `analysis_run_ids` and a bounded `validation_state`/validation-run pointer. Store immutable full comparisons in a `DataStructureBase`/`Field` analysis sidecar with declared fields, references, form contract and stable document identity. No raw Firestore-dictionary shortcut.

Each field comparison records: target/entity IDs; P/C/V run IDs and value hashes; match/equivalence rule version; substantive/trivial agreement; disagreement type; evidence image/text hashes and region/span pointers; verifier verdict; proposed correction; resolution attempt/cost; final status/reason; certificate/policy version; applicability/coverage and reference-evaluation status where relevant. Book/field status must distinguish `auto_accepted`, `auto_corrected`, `abstained`, `experimental` and `legacy_unknown`. Do not set `validated_by` to a fictitious human or redefine an old human-validation flag silently.

Preserve `Page.extraction_evidence`, `Book.metadata_evidence_payloads`/`metadata_history`, `Character.detection_audit`, `Alias.provenance` and immutable alias-review history. Every record needs origin, **provider, requested/resolved model and convention version**, plus prompts/schema/settings, source hashes, run/request IDs and timestamps. Derived fields link all contributing sources. Human values use `not_applicable` for provider/model and retain their history; missing historical provenance stays explicitly unknown.

Use the entity layer's bundled/precondition update mechanism to commit accepted value, provenance, status and timestamp together. Ordinary separate Field assignments are write-through and can leave partial history. Validate identity before creating title-derived book IDs or author/publisher links; later renaming requires the existing controlled migration, not a casual title edit. This publication path and removal of existing UI approval gates require future implementation and qualification; this document does not claim today's app already runs unattended.

### What runs and what is scored

For each book, retain original input bytes and order; immutable references and their provenance; P's raw outputs; blind C's raw outputs; V/adjudication outputs when the tested policy invokes them; final accepted/abstained output; and metered attempts. Scoring never sends human truth to either model. Run the primary's real preprocessing, while preserving originals for counterchecks: sharing a mistaken crop with both providers can create shared error. Report shared-preprocessing versus original-image verification explicitly.

First score P and C independently against eligible reference, then their agreement, then V and the final policy. Define the unit per endpoint in advance (book identifier tuple, text-bearing page/span, known character/alias attachment); cluster all uncertainty at book/work level. Report numerator, denominator and reference tier for every metric, not just percentages. Use a fixed reference-positive denominator for recall, including missed outputs. For output-based groundedness show emitted count, empty-output count and recall alongside it.

For reference-evaluable, nontrivial opportunities, let:

- `E` = P is wrong under the frozen field-specific correctness rule; `K` = P is correct.
- `A` = P and C agree under the frozen substantive equivalence rule; `D` = substantive disagreement; `X` = unavailable/failed/incomparable checker outcome. These are disjoint. An unsupported reference is `U`, outside correctness denominators but inside coverage reporting.
- `n(S)` = count of opportunities in event `S` (or the explicitly stated weighted count). Do not silently mix unit types or reference tiers.

| Quantity | Definition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement rate on content | `n(A) / n(A ∪ D)` plus `X` rate over all eligible attempts | Similarity only; report trivial-agreement share separately over all field opportunities. |
| Correctness given agreement | `n(A ∩ K) / n(A)` on known truth | How often agreement predicts verified primary correctness; often called negative predictive value when disagreement is the alarm. |
| Shared-error risk among agreements | `n(A ∩ E) / n(A)` | Residual risk of trusting agreement; complement of the preceding quantity on the same known-truth denominator. Also report `n(A ∩ E) / n(eligible known-truth attempts)`. |
| **False-negative rate of agreement-as-validator** | **`n(A ∩ E) / n(E)`** | Fraction of genuine primary errors missed because the providers agreed. Report `X ∩ E` separately as blocked/unresolved errors. Detection by substantive disagreement is `n(D ∩ E)/n(E)`, not necessarily `1 − FNR` when X exists. |
| Disagreement yield | `n(D ∩ E) / n(D)` | Fraction of alarms finding a real primary error. |
| False-alarm rate | `n(D ∩ K) / n(K)` | Fraction of correct primary outputs that the checker unnecessarily challenges. Also report false alarms per 100 attempted books. |
| Wasted-alarm share | `n(D ∩ K) / n(D)` | Fraction of disagreements consuming resources without finding a primary error. |

Expand disagreements into **P wrong/C right; P right/C wrong; both wrong differently; both defensibly correct under multiple valid answers**. The last case diagnoses comparator/convention noise. Record unknown-truth disagreements separately; don't label them primary errors. In an agreement, shared correctness remains unmeasurable if the reference does not establish it. Both models omitting a reference-known alias is a shared recovery error, even if their emitted alias sets match perfectly.

For V, repeat the table with `pass / flag / abstain`. Let `I` mean V was invoked: **`FNR_V|I = n(I ∩ pass ∩ E)/n(I ∩ E)`**, and residual risk is `n(I ∩ pass ∩ E)/n(I ∩ pass)`. These describe the selected invoked population, not all books/claims. The all-claim Tune20 comparison supports a development estimate across its eligible claims; a broader estimate from selectively invoked checks requires frozen, nonzero inclusion probabilities and appropriate selection weights. Neither is a substitute for held-out policy evaluation.

For the complete deployed policy, define **`FNR_policy = n(E ∩ final_wrong ∩ accepted)/n(E)`** over **all eligible, known-truth primary errors**, including erroneous agreements that bypass V. Repaired or abstained primary errors are contained; final accepted errors are missed. Separately measure `wrong accepted / all accepted`, errors prevented or repaired per attempt, **correct-primary → wrong-final** changes, abstention and accepted coverage. Define a policy false alarm as any substantive claim that P is erroneous when P is correct, whether later cleared or not; routine scheduled checks that support P are not alarms. Report false-alarm rate over all known-correct P opportunities, plus correct outputs unnecessarily abstained or changed. An error corrected before acceptance is not a final error; an uncorrected error merely waved through by an adjudicator is. Publish book-level “any material error” rates alongside opportunity metrics.

Quantify checker costs: USD and latency per true error detected/corrected; paid checks and escalation dollars spent on `D ∩ K`; correct outputs turned into abstentions; harmful corrections; human minutes actually used in Tune only. With zero detected errors, cost per finding is undefined, with total cost still reported. A high false-alarm checker can make autonomy unusable even when it is cautious. Compare final risk/coverage/cost against P alone and against blind C; select only from development results.

The calibration report must include all agreements, not only inspected disagreements. Reference scoring of already verified agreements supplies the otherwise missing shared-error denominator without fresh annotation. Report any available rare-name, non-English, stylised typography, illustration-lettering and implicit-attribution subgroup separately; an absent/unknown subgroup cannot receive a general certificate. No supplied measurement establishes the FNR yet: this experiment measures it where the reference supports truth.

## 5. The autonomy gate and strength of proof

Freeze one primary + validator + resolver + abstention policy before proof. Predeclare primary endpoints, normalization, material-error definition, handling of unknown references, sampling weights, subgroups, confidence procedures and cost/coverage stopping rules. Keep challenge/synthetic fixtures separate from population estimates. No favourable-seed selection, repeated peeking, optional sample extension after a miss or replacing failed books.

**Per-field release requires all of the following:**

1. Final accepted error/recovery bounds and coverage satisfy §2; known structural/fixture regressions are absent. Aggregate scores cannot compensate for a failed field or a failed declared subgroup.
2. Where acceptance relies on agreement/V passing, the calibrated residual error meets that field's error budget. Additionally propose **complete-policy FNR ≤5%** and **policy false-alarm rate ≤5%**, each with an upper confidence bound using the all-opportunity denominators defined in §4. The former includes missed errors on agreements bypassing V; the latter limits needless escalation/abstention. Report standalone agreement FNR and invocation-conditional V FNR as diagnostics, never substitutes for the deployed policy's gate. Report these thresholds separately from substantive field accuracy.
3. Reference completeness and independence support the precise claim. For unknown accepted truths, show best/worst-case sensitivity (unknowns all correct versus all wrong); never omit a large unknown fraction and call the remainder whole-field accuracy. Known-positive recall or M1 alone cannot graduate an identity/speaker field.
4. The **tested final correction path** passes too; no untested resolver, implicit-model upgrade or new normalizer is introduced after proof. Evidence/provenance completeness, replay/correction capability, budget limits and no-routine-review publication are qualified offline and in the bounded operational ramp.

If there are no verified primary errors, **FNR is undefined, not zero**. A very accurate primary can make detector sensitivity impossible to estimate. In that situation an agreement-dependent autonomy claim does not pass; a separately predeclared direct-final-risk proof could justify the field without claiming the validator has established sensitivity. It still needs sufficient independent evidence and cannot be substituted after seeing a disappointing FNR denominator.

**Uncertainty and sample limits:** use stratified book/work-cluster intervals for ratios and paired effects, preserving sampling weights. A finite-population correction describes the sampled pilot pool only, not future books. Use a conservative exact book-event bound for rare/zero errors; a bootstrap of all-zero observations must not return a spurious zero-width certificate. For simultaneous release claims, freeze the number `m` of primary gates and use one-sided alpha `0.05/m` or a specified equivalent family-wise method; exploratory subgroup intervals do not silently become release tests.

For orientation, the following are **arithmetic sizing examples**, assuming independent representative books, perfect reference and a single unadjusted one-sided 95% endpoint. With zero errors, the upper error bound is `1 − 0.05^(1/n)`:

| Independent books with zero failures | Upper future-book error bound |
|---|---:|
| Proposed Prove60 | **4.8703%** |
| Remaining121, if hypothetically all untouched | **2.4454%** |
| All161, if hypothetically all untouched | **1.8435%** |

To bound book error below 1%, 0.5% or 0.1% this way takes respectively **299, 598 or 2,995 zero-failure independent books**; simultaneous gates require more. A 5% FNR upper bound with no misses needs **59 independent genuinely erroneous books**, not 59 arbitrary books. Aliases/tokens/pages within the same book cannot be counted as independent replacements, and the 178 recorded aliases do not guarantee 178 evaluable errors.

Consequently the 161-book calibration is cheap and essential but **not enough, by itself, for the recommended rare-error guarantees**. It can reject poor validators, quantify available error detection, expose false alarms, tune the pipeline and establish corpus-specific results. Prove60 is an honest independent first test, not a promised passing certificate. If evidence is insufficient, publish “not established” and a prospective evidence requirement; do not relax thresholds to make credits appear successful. No large annotation programme is reinstated by this design. More API calls on the same books cannot repair missing truth or increase the number of independent books.

## 6. Run with no routine human review

**Drift checks.** Pin model identifiers where supported, record actual returned model and provider/request metadata, and treat deprecation/substitution as a change requiring qualification. Silent provider updates remain possible even under a stable name. Freeze a **16-book canary panel** from exposed, scored material: eight fixed regression books and eight rotating coverage books, including content-bearing hard cases and separate wordless controls. This is a proposed monitoring size, not a statistical guarantee; include only available reference-supported endpoints and name missing strata.

Before the first new production work after a model/prompt/schema/convention/processing change, before resuming after more than 30 idle days, and after **50 processed books or 30 active days, whichever comes first**, rerun the relevant stages and score automatically. Idle cycles require no repetitive spending; resumption requires a current check. Bound the panel's cost before dispatch, defer production if it cannot fit. Fixed-panel small text/crop checks run first; full stages affected by a change still need validation. Record baseline stochastic variability during Tune, then freeze alarm limits before Run: any new critical fixture error, unexpected model, or material movement beyond those limits suspends the affected field. Repeatedly rerunning until a green result is prohibited.

Monitor the automatic 5% agreement checks (§3), disagreement, failed-output, resolver and abstention rates against frozen stratum-specific control limits. These are automated signals, **not fresh human accuracy labels**. Canaries detect regressions on known inputs, not every shared error on new books. If canaries fail, a maintainer investigates the incident and requalifies the policy. That exceptional maintenance is distinct from routine book-by-book validation. No monthly human audit sample is required by this proposal; absent new human truth, disclose the remaining blind spot and the exposure between checks.

**Distribution shift.** Admit only inputs meeting the photographed-session manifest and certified scope: complete source count/order, unchanged stock-camera pixel/orientation handling, adequate resolution/focus, qualified page/layout ranges, covered languages/scripts and supported content types. Use deterministic file/quality statistics plus a calibrated language/layout/risk classifier; compare page counts, text density, script mix, blur/crop and evidence/abstention distributions with the pilot ranges. The classifier's false-negative rate is itself unverified: test it with existing exposed examples and controlled corruptions before relying on it. Unseen scripts, extreme layouts, failed completeness checks and unsupported image transformations trigger abstention, not a silent English/default interpretation. No OOD detector guarantees finding every semantic novelty; future-population claims remain restricted accordingly.

The [photo-ingestion design](photo_ingestion_design_2026-11-05.md) deliberately retains the stock camera to protect the distribution behind the ~0.99 OCR result. Do not substitute a second camera, lossy re-encoding or changed crop/resolution without a paired qualification. Source admission/session closure and separator/book boundaries must be proven independently of text agreement; two models cannot detect a page that neither received. Existing manual endpoint/title confirmation is an implementation gap until its replacement has passed those gates.

**Detectability and repair of silent errors.** Retain authorised original/source images, upload/transformation digests, page order and region coordinates; raw P/C/V responses; exact text revisions and span offsets; model/prompt/convention/pricing versions; final acceptance reason, calibrated risk stratum and certificate; and every derived-field dependency. Model self-confidence is stored as a feature, not presented as calibrated probability. Maintain a queryable index from model/input/convention version to affected records and derived exports.

If a shared error is discovered later, locate the entire affected cohort, invalidate dependent acceptance/certificates, rerun only affected stages, and issue a new version with before/after history and research-export revision. Use update preconditions so repairs cannot overwrite newer human amendments. Never erase the earlier evidence or silently alter published analysis. Retention makes correction possible, not inevitable: some undetected errors may remain forever. The certificate therefore states bounded measured risk, not zero error. Storage/access/retention must follow existing policy and carry infrastructure costs; absent retained evidence, re-derivability cannot be promised.

**Operational interruption is allowed.** If the checker, account, source inventory or canary is unavailable, checkpoint and abstain/pause affected publication. Do not fall back to unchecked primary output, another key or a human approval dependency. Publish separate per-field availability and end-to-end completion so reliability failures are visible. The user may choose to widen scope later; excluded cases are not silently declared successful.
