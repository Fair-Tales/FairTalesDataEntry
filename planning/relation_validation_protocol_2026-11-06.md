This establishes only partial local feasibility, not alignment to the current photo corpus. Resolve repeated strings by full context and source evidence, not nearest match. Differences involving missing text, sentence splitting, quotation or changed OCR require source inspection. Unresolved cases stay in the alignment-failure denominator; do not selectively discard difficult/wrong cases.

The 650 speech SIDs were generated by historical sentence extraction/order. ## 3. Interface and blinding order

The interface is an offline annotation design, not a UI implemented by this revision. It should show the source image/PDF region, adjacent pages and expandable full-book context; stable neutral character IDs and the independently human-coded inventory/aliases where available; and an option to add a missing entity or select unknown, narrator, collective, non-character or no recipient. Never force a name from an incomplete extractor inventory. If neutral text is displayed, retain image access and provenance; machine OCR must not be passed off as human text. Humans correct source spans from the image before using them as truth.

For each section record speech/not-speech, direct/indirect, exact utterance span, speaker ID or admissible set, recipient ID or admissible set, explicit/implicit evidence, ambiguity, evidence regions, and a brief reason. Allow multiple recipients and “not recoverable from evidence”. Preserve literal evidence and normalised labels separately. A wrong answer requires corrected identity/set or explicit unresolved status. Plural/collective and addressed-to versus narrated-about conventions are frozen in a codebook with exposed examples. Language addressed to a character, own speech and narration about a character remain separate outcomes; speaker/recipient success does not certify an unlabelled narration-about relation.

Order matters:

1. **Freeze sampling, conventions and analysis.** Record exposure, source hashes, alignment, exact independent-work/field counts, margins, model families and annotated-output schema. Tune both provider policies on development material and choose the release candidate there. Freeze the new 200 source units and the random audit selection procedure before proof outputs, within the complete source frame and SRS design above. The random 50 is a simple random sample without replacement of the 200; hide its membership from extractors and model annotators.
2. **Freeze predictions.** Run both extractors independently with their own OCR and inventory for the autonomous arm. Seal raw outputs, failures, abstentions and hashes. Separate diagnostic arms using common PDF text/human inventory may explain losses but are not the autonomous result. Neither extractor sees old flags, new labels or the other's output.
3. **Model annotation, blind to outputs.** Astra on ultra labels all 200 directly from source under the frozen codebook. If an OpenAI extractor is being evaluated using a cross-family proxy, an Anthropic annotator independently labels the same 200. Neither sees extractor answers, old flags, the other's annotations or human responses. Freeze both label sets before revealing calibration results. These must be fresh isolated annotation contexts; this planning agent's knowledge of historical flags must not be carried into the annotating context.
4. **Human independent pass on random 50.** A designated human sees source/context and the neutral inventory, but no model labels, extractor outputs, confidence, old correctness flags or provider identity. No prefilled answers, default-correct checkboxes or candidate suggestions. Save and lock the independent labels before displaying any AI result. Human knowledge of source coding is recorded; independence here means labels were not induced by predictions, not that the human is infallible.
5. **Only after locking truth, derive the historical flags.** Present anonymised, order-randomised outputs for each provider if needed to map the locked answer to wrong/ambiguous/indirect/highlighting flags. Record corrected speaker and recipient from the locked source pass. Preserve both strict single-answer and admissible-set/ambiguity outcomes; do not revise truth simply to agree with a provider. Genuine source/codebook mistakes require documented adjudication with original labels retained. Retuning the codebook or extraction on these labels makes affected material development evidence.
6. **Score and report.** Reveal model–human discrepancies and provider identities only after locking all requisite labels. Compute both extractors against the **same human 50**. Keep human labels, historical accepted flags and model-only labels in separate provenance tiers. Cross-provider label agreement or majority vote never upgrades a tier.

The human interface must have a progress/save mechanism, immutable initial-label history, source zoom/context and stable IDs. Export source IDs/hashes, spans, answers, evidence, annotator type/family, timestamp, independence status, uncertainty and revision history. Do not store secrets or call services from the annotation UI design. Scheduling, sampling, crosswalk generation, interface implementation, deterministic scoring, reporting and preparation of discrepancy bundles can all be agent work without circularity.

## 4. What the 200-model / 50-human compromise supports

**Astra cannot be independent of an OpenAI-family extractor.** “Ultra”, a different prompt or another model in the same family does not solve this. Astra labels can be a cross-family proxy for Anthropic extraction; an independently run Anthropic annotator can be a cross-family proxy for OpenAI extraction. This implies up to **400 model annotations**, not just Astra's 200, in addition to the two extraction arms. Different providers can still share source/OCR, inventory, conventions and training errors. Crossing families reduces one channel; it does not create human truth.

Use the **same random human 50** to measure both annotators' exact/admissible-set agreement, confusion, ambiguity, missingness and errors by relation/stratum, and to compare both extractors directly. Do not pick a provider by comparing its performance against a different proxy reference from its competitor. A useful model-assisted pilot rule is observed ≥0.96 agreement for each relation on evaluable human cases, with no demonstrated systematic identity/ambiguity error; this is an operational rule for retaining proxy labels, **not a 96% accuracy certificate**. Report exact counts and uncertainty even if that rule passes. Low agreement discards the model-only labels for inferential use; 50 full attribution labels have then been spent discovering the problem, **plus separately recorded source-screening/alignment time**. Do not present this as a total 50-section human-time budget.

Even **50/50** perfect agreement gives an independent-unit one-sided 95% annotation-error upper bound of **5.8155%**; 49/50 gives a lower agreement bound about **0.9086**. Clustered future-work inference can be worse. For the fixed 200 frame under simple random auditing, zero discrepancies in 50 still permits **10 errors among the remaining 150** at the one-sided 95% finite-population bound—**6.67% of the unaudited 150**. Thus the remaining labels can be used with a measured error estimate and uncertainty, not treated as 150 extra human truths. A ≥95% lower-bound annotator-accuracy gate is impossible with 50 even when perfect under the simple iid model.

Report three outputs:

- **Human-only comparison on the random 50**, with source/provenance and work-cluster uncertainty for generalisation, and design-based uncertainty for the fixed 200 frame.
- **Model-assisted estimate on the 200**, explicitly labelled. For a binary extractor score, an optional prespecified difference estimator is `mean(proxy-scored outcomes on 200) + mean(human score − proxy score on random 50)`. It estimates the fixed-frame human-scored mean under the random audit, with uncertainty from the correction sample and its sampling design; no independence of extractor and annotator errors is assumed. The provider difference uses paired corrections on the same 50. Do not report naive n=200 binomial precision. Unequal probabilities require the corresponding weighted estimator.
- **Sensitivity bounds** using the measured proxy-label error. A joint lower bound such as proxy performance minus annotation-error upper bound needs a stated error allocation (for example alpha 0.025 to each component); even zero/50 then gives annotation-error upper **7.11%** before clustering. Pointwise 95% ingredients are not automatically a 95% combined claim. Missing/unresolved human truths require best/worst-case sensitivity, not dropped denominators.

This is scientifically useful as an economical **annotation-feasibility and model-assisted audit**. It does **not** establish non-inferiority to human annotators, a human accuracy ceiling, independent human IAA, or the precision of 200 human-annotated sections. Machine-only labels cannot certify the model family that made them. As a transparent pilot with random human validation, uncertainty and a reproducible codebook it could survive peer review; presented as 200 independent gold labels or proof of human equivalence it would not. Cross-family annotation is worth testing, but the proposed compromise is unsound as a guaranteed substitute for human certification.

**Staged human time:** recommend 50 as a feasibility pilot, with a predeclared option to independently label the remaining 150 if proof is required. In the recommended design, the 50 analysis is exploratory and there is **one confirmatory release analysis at 200 human sections** if that stage is undertaken, on unchanged frozen predictions. No release claim is made by repeatedly testing at 50,100,150,200. If the owner instead wants a possible release decision at 50, predeclare a sequentially valid procedure/alpha spending before any labels; do not retrospectively use a favourable unadjusted 50 result and then extend only failures. Keeping labels at 200 model/50 human still yields the useful measured-error outputs above, with certification explicitly unresolved where bounds fail.

## 5. Non-inferiority, improvement and coverage

The published results become **floors to beat**, rather than targets to reproduce. Freeze these as separate claims:

1. **Historical-benchmark non-inferiority:** proposed margins are **3 percentage points speaker** and **3.5 points recipient**. Against fixed historical anchors 0.97/0.945, require lower bounds **>0.94 / >0.91** (non-inferiority null at or below the margin). This is a comparison to declared research benchmarks; the anchors are themselves historical estimates under different inputs. It is not a statistical comparison of two randomly sampled systems, nor non-inferiority to human annotation.
2. **Paired historical-system comparison where feasible:** on source units with frozen aligned historical predictions and new corrected human truth, score both legacy and new outputs against that same truth. Require the one-sided cluster lower bound for `accuracy_new − accuracy_legacy` to exceed **−0.03 / −0.035**. Preserve both systems' failures/abstentions, sampling weights and book clusters. Archived wrong flags alone cannot supply corrected labels. If common units are too few or exposed, report a development diagnostic; do not invent a paired proof or force GPT-4o reruns to fill it.
3. **Improvement objective:** point estimates must **exceed 0.97 /0.945**, alongside coverage. A point-estimate win is not statistically established superiority; that stronger claim needs a paired lower bound above zero. Failure to beat the historical point floors is not rescued by mere non-inferiority. Current frontiers must earn their additional cost through measured results.

For the recommended confirmatory 200-human analysis, primary attribution bounds are **exact design-based finite-frame bounds**, inverted from the section SRS hypergeometric distribution with known `N`. Use these for the 0.94/0.91 historical-benchmark tests, alongside point estimates exceeding 0.97/0.945. Freeze strict/admissible-set scoring and unknown-truth sensitivity before analysis. A fully labelled finite-frame census reports its exact score; it has no sampling error for that frame, but human-label uncertainty remains.

For any paired historical comparison, state its common-unit frame and inclusion design. An exposed convenience overlap is a development diagnostic; a probability-sampled aligned overlap may support a design-based paired analysis under its declared design. Report work-cluster intervals for broader transfer as a separate analysis; use the main design's bounded-score boundary safeguard where appropriate. The historical paper's section accuracy, this finite-frame section accuracy, and future-work macro accuracy are three different quantities. **Passing the finite-frame gate establishes conditional performance within the measured cohort, not unrestricted future-book autonomy or human-equivalent performance.** Future-book release needs a separately supported transport claim, and any stronger numerical future-work floor must pass its own bounds. If it does not, future-book relation outputs remain experimental/abstained; money or extra proxy labels cannot change that conclusion.

All primary relation scores retain failures/abstentions in intention-to-process/joint recovery reports. Accepted-only accuracy is separate and must meet the unchanged **95% opportunity and 90% required-book operational coverage floors**. Define ambiguous truth in advance: score a uniquely supported identity under strict accuracy; show set-valued correctness separately; unresolved truth is unknown, with sensitivity and coverage. Do not delete ambiguous recipients to inflate accuracy or silently count any model answer as correct. Report explicit/implicit, direct/indirect, collective/narrator/unknown, source tier, OCR quality and inventory-available/missing strata. Sparse strata remain uncertified.

Measure speech detection and source-to-correct-relation recovery on the independent source frame, alongside attribution conditional on known speech/inventory. Own speech and language addressed to characters can be certified only for their labelled scope; narration about characters requires its own relation labels and remains unsupported until present. Non-inferiority to the historical conditional system is not an end-to-end autonomy certificate. Report two provider arms and all failures, select the candidate on Tune, and require fresh confirmation if a different winner is chosen after proof.

## 6. The unresolved human-agreement question

The combined Sophie/Kate workbook is readable, but **whether the same sentences were independently coded remains UNVERIFIED**. One combined answer per row could be a partitioned assignment, a reconciliation, or a merged selection from overlapping originals. It cannot distinguish them.

The decisive check is to recover the **original coder-specific, pre-reconciliation sheets/exports**, retaining coder IDs, sentence code, verified book/text and initial labels; join on verified source identity; count the overlap; and confirm from records or the coders that each completed initial labels **without seeing the other's**. Examine missingness and exclusions before computing agreement. Numerical code overlap alone is insufficient.

- **If independent overlap exists:** compute speech raw agreement, confusion, prevalence and kappa, plus speaker exact/set agreement after identity normalisation, with book-cluster intervals. Retain the original disagreements rather than reconciled values. This estimates human agreement on that sampled task and can inform a human-comparison margin; it is not error-free truth or a universal accuracy ceiling. The workbook has no recipient column, so it still supplies no recipient IAA.

The new human 50/200 study supports model–human-reference performance; a human-ceiling claim would require a separately planned second blind human overlap.
